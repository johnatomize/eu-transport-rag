## Built and verified

- **Repo and tracking:** private GitHub repo, with milestones, tracking issues and sub-issues for checkpoints A–D on the [board](https://github.com/users/johnatomize/projects/2).
- **Secret scanning (#4):** a gitleaks pre-commit hook guards every commit since the first. Before the first commit, a staged fake token was refused.
- **Python tooling (#5):** a `uv` project on Python 3.12 with ruff and pytest, locked in `uv.lock`. `make check` runs lint, tests and a gitleaks scan of the full history, and passes on a fresh clone with only `uv` and gitleaks installed.
- **Local Postgres + pgvector (#7):** `docker-compose.yml` runs `pgvector/pgvector` 0.8.7 on Postgres 18, pinned by tag and digest, with credentials from `.env` and the port bound to localhost only. `make db-up` waits until it is healthy, `make db-check` runs `CREATE EXTENSION vector` and prints `pgvector 0.8.7`, and data survives `make db-down` / `make db-up`. Docker runs in Colima.
- **Corpus manifest and fetch (#11):** `corpus/manifest.yaml` pins the 20 phase 1 documents (three acts, ten guidance notes, seven clarification notes) by sha256, and their 2.0 MB of raw files are committed under `corpus/raw/`. `make corpus` checks them without network. `scripts/fetch_corpus.py --refetch` downloaded all 20 again from CELLAR and the Commission site on 27 October 2026, with identical hashes.
- **Parsing the acts (#12):** `make parse` turns the three acts into 716 nodes, each with a stable ID such as `561/2006:art6(3)`, its heading path, its amendment markers as metadata and its cross-references (ID scheme in `DECISIONS.md`). The counts are 561/2006: 33 articles, 76 paragraphs and 93 points; 165/2014: 48 articles, 148 paragraphs, 212 points and two annexes in 35 parts; 2002/15: 16 articles, rebuilt from flat HTML. Of 318 cross-references, 260 resolve to a node and 55 point to acts outside the corpus. The other 3 are listed: relative references inside 165/2014 Art. 45 that point into 561/2006. The only unparsed content is 9 images, mostly the tachograph pictograms, which stay as `[image]` in the text. A committed snapshot of every ID guards against ID changes.
- **Guidance notes (#13):** `make parse` also turns the 17 guidance and clarification notes into 47 nodes: one per note and one per page (30 pages, such as `guidance-7:p3`), with about 56,000 characters of text (library and cleaning in `DECISIONS.md`). Footnote markers and running headers are removed. 14 notes interpret a single act in the corpus (561/2006 or 165/2014), and their references without an act point into it; guidance-4, guidance-5 and clarification-7 cite either acts outside the corpus or more than one act. The notes' 88 references resolve as follows: 68 to a node, 9 to acts outside the corpus, and 11 left unresolved, each checked by hand and listed in the tests. Images (guidance-4, guidance-7, guidance-10) and pages drawn as a diagram, table or form (guidance-7 pp. 5–6, guidance-9 p. 3, guidance-10 p. 5) are listed as not understood. On guidance-10 p. 5 the pairing of nation codes and countries is lost.
- **Dates of the guidance notes (#26):** every note in the manifest has a publication date with its source (a Commission letter dated in the note, or the PDF's creation date), and `make parse` puts it on the note node. Only guidance-9 and guidance-10 are newer than the 2020 Mobility Package (sources and reasons in `DECISIONS.md`).
- **Question schema and validator (#15):** `data/questions.v1.jsonl` holds the evaluation questions, one JSON object per line, with the fields and rules in `DECISIONS.md`. `make questions`, which `make check` runs, checks every row against the schema and the parsed corpus, and fails if the file is missing. It rejects malformed rows, unknown or repeated fields, key facts missing from the answer, gold node IDs that are not in the corpus, are too broad or sit in an amending article, and rows that break the rule for their question type. Each message names the line, the question ID and the field. It prints a table of question types against split and phrasing.
- **Question set (#16):** 100 questions. They are split 60 dev and 40 test by a seeded shuffle within each type and phrasing, frozen from now on (method in `DECISIONS.md`). The validator also fails if a type has no questions in either split, and a test pins the 40 test questions. Key facts are written with digits, such as `9 hours` and `7.5 tonnes`.

  - **Gold nodes:** 55 questions have gold nodes in 561/2006, 17 in 165/2014 and 14 in 2002/15. 13 cite a guidance note, and for 8 of them the note is the only evidence.
  - **Version traps:** 11 come from the 2020 Mobility Package, 1 from Reg. 165/2014 and 2 from Reg. 2024/1258. Each old answer was checked against an earlier text of the act.   - **Unanswerable questions:** each was confirmed absent by a keyword search over the parsed text, and its notes record the search terms.
- **Gold answers checked (#17):** the owner checked all 100 gold answers against the text on a review page built by `scripts/gold_review.py`, which shows each question next to its gold nodes, the text that introduces a list item, and the whole article. Four were corrected (q003, q006, q008 and q033). For q062 and q097, whose gold nodes are list items, the fix is to show the introducing text on the page and carry it in chunks (#33). The validator now fails on any question not checked by the owner.
- **Chunking (#33):** `make chunks` cuts the corpus three ways and writes each to `build/chunks-<strategy>.jsonl`: 133 article chunks (mean 284 words, with 165/2014's annexes split into their parts), 412 paragraph chunks (mean 112) and 246 windows of 200 words overlapping by 50 (mean 216 with the prefix). Every chunk starts with its heading path, and a list item carries the text that introduces it, such as "For the purposes of this Regulation the following definitions shall apply:" above each definition in 561/2006 Art. 4. Tests check that every piece of text is in some chunk apart from the amending articles and the acts' titles, that every gold node of the question set can be found in each strategy, and that IDs are the same across runs (choices in `DECISIONS.md`).
- **Retrieval metrics (#35):** `src/rag/retrieval_eval.py` scores any ranked list of chunks against the gold nodes, following the rules in `DECISIONS.md` ("how retrieval evaluation scores a ranking"). It gives recall@1, @3, @5 and @10, fractional recall, MRR@10 and nDCG@10, each with a 95% interval, overall, by phrasing and by question type, and the number of unanswerable questions left out. The Wilson interval matches four published values (Newcombe 1998), the bootstrap gives the same interval for the same seed, and scoring the test split fails unless an explicit flag meant for checkpoint G is set.
- **Keyword search (#34):** BM25 over the chunks of any strategy, with tokens that keep act numbers, article references and decimals whole (choices in `DECISIONS.md`). `make search Q="…"` prints the best five chunks for a typed question. "Can I take my weekly rest in the truck cab?" finds 561/2006 Art. 8(6) first; "How long may I drive in a day?" misses Art. 6(1), because the act says "daily driving time". That vocabulary gap is what E's embeddings have to close.

- **Embedding cost estimate (#44):** `make embed-dry-run` estimates, without calling AWS, what embedding the corpus and the questions with Titan Text Embeddings V2 will cost, from the model and price in `config/embedding.yaml`. The three chunkings and the 100 questions come to about 192,000 tokens, counting text shared between chunkings once: US$0.0040, or US$0.0061 with a 50% margin. Bedrock runs in Stockholm, where the price read on 31 October 2026 is a tenth of Frankfurt's (reasons in `DECISIONS.md`). It fails if any input is empty or, with the margin, may be over the model's limit (the longest is about 2,860 tokens of 8,192), and warns when the price is more than a week old.
- **Hybrid search (#48):** `RRF` in `src/rag/retrieve.py` merges the rankings of any retrievers over the same chunks by reciprocal rank fusion (constant 60, each ranking read 50 deep; choices in `DECISIONS.md`). Tests with fixed rankings show that a chunk ranked second by both methods beats one ranked first by only one, that a chunk found by only one still appears, that ties fall in chunk order, and that chunks BM25 pads its top k with, because they hold no query word, get no credit.
- **Embedding client and cache (#46):** `make embed` reports how many of the 831 distinct chunk and question texts already have a vector, and what sending the rest would cost; it sends nothing. `make embed SEND=1` (now `RUN=1`) pays, but refuses to start if a text may be over the model's limit, if the price is more than a week old, or if the estimate, margin included, would take spending past the approved cap, counting paid runs not yet in the spend log. Vectors are cached in `embeddings/`, which is committed, keyed by model, vector size, normalisation and text, so a text is paid for once and a changed setting never reuses an old vector. A paid run logs the token count Bedrock returns for each call in `runs/embeddings/`, for checking the spend log against the bill. Tests use a stand-in for Bedrock: a cached text makes no call, a changed setting misses the cache, a failed call caches nothing, and a run over the cap refuses to start. The real Bedrock call was never made: the project's Bedrock limit for Titan is 0, and #57 moved E to a local model.
- **Vector search (#47):** search by cosine similarity, in memory from the embedding cache or in the local pgvector database, behind the same interface as BM25 (choices in `DECISIONS.md`). `make vector-index` loads each chunking's cached vectors into one table, replacing that chunking's rows in one step; until #50 pays for the vectors it refuses and leaves the database as it was. Search is exact: no approximate index is used, or can be built on the table. `make test-db` runs the tests that need the database. They show that both backends give identical hits and scores on vectors whose sums are exact, ties in chunk order, and agree to float32 precision on 300 random vectors of Titan's size. They also show that loading again gives the same rows, a load that fails partway changes nothing, an index whose chunks or vectors no longer match is refused and the query plan uses no approximate index. `make check` stays offline.
- **Cross-reference expansion (#49):** `Expansion` in `src/rag/retrieve.py` wraps any retriever and puts the chunks a hit refers to right after it, inside the top k (choices in `DECISIONS.md`). `make search Q="…" REFS=1` shows it: for "What counts as other work for a driver who also works for another employer?" the definition of "other work" in 561/2006 Art. 4(e) brings in the definition of working time in Directive 2002/15 Art. 3(a). Tests on a hand-built corpus show that a referenced chunk comes in right after the chunk that cites it, that references outside the corpus, unresolved ones and chapters bring in nothing, and that nothing is added twice. On the corpus, 561/2006 Art. 6(5) brings in the definition of periods of availability in 2002/15 Art. 3(b).   - **Hybrid search is the best of the three with every chunking.** With paragraph chunks it finds eight more questions in the top 5 than BM25 and loses none; with article chunks, four more and one fewer. The intervals still overlap (paragraph chunks: 0.60, 0.46–0.72, against 0.76, 0.63–0.86), so 50 questions don't prove it. The clearest gain is at the very top: recall@1 rises from 0.30–0.32 to 0.42–0.46, and MRR from 0.61–0.67 to 0.75–0.78.
  - **Meaning search closes much of the vocabulary gap.** On paraphrased questions recall@5 is 0.76, 0.71 and 0.61 with hybrid search against BM25's 0.66, 0.55 and 0.53. Vector search alone matches BM25 with article chunks and beats it with the shorter ones, and its top 5 article chunks hold 1,648 words against BM25's 2,348: it favours short, focused articles.
  - **Keywords still matter for version traps.** Vector search alone finds 0.88, 0.75 and 0.62 of them against BM25's 1.00, 1.00 and 0.75, because an act's number and year are exact words. Hybrid search keeps BM25's 1.00 with article and paragraph chunks, which is why it is ahead overall.
  - **Expansion is still noise overall**, as with BM25: between −2 and +3 questions. It helps vector search with article chunks most (0.72 → 0.78, recall@10 0.80 → 0.88). Cross-act questions stay at 0.00–0.33 for every run.
  - **Five of the six questions every BM25 run missed are now found in the top 10 by at least one run.** q023 ("What is a 'break'…?") is in the top 5 of every vector run and first with paragraph chunks and windows. q051 (the empty truck to the car wash) is found by expansion with all three methods. q050 (a Swedish company's responsibility for a breach in Germany) is in the top 5 only with vector search over windows. q066 (who trains drivers on the tachograph) is found only by vector search over articles with expansion, and q062 (56 hours with loading work) only reaches the top 10, with hybrid search over articles. q048, "I also work evenings in a warehouse for another company. Do I have to put that on my tachograph?", is missed by every run: neither method connects a warehouse job with the act's "other work".
  - **A paired count is sharper than the intervals.** On the questions where hybrid and BM25 over paragraph chunks disagree at recall@5, hybrid wins 8 and loses 0. If neither method were better, a split that lopsided would happen with a probability of 0.008 (a two-sided sign test). With article chunks it is 4 to 1 (0.38) and with windows 5 to 2 (0.45). Nine such comparisons were looked at, all on the dev split, so this is a strong hint for G to test, not a result.
  - **At an equal budget of words, hybrid search over paragraph chunks finds the most.** Taking chunks in rank order until 1,000 words of chunk text (headings included) are used, hybrid search over paragraph chunks has all the evidence for 37 of the 50 questions, vector search 36, BM25 32; BM25 over article chunks has 20. It leads at 500 and 1,500 words too; at 3,000 words the runs find between 34 and 40.
  - **Similarity doesn't separate unanswerable questions.** The best paragraph chunk scores a cosine of 0.51–0.66 for the 10 unanswerable dev questions and 0.58–0.89 for the 50 answerable ones (mean 0.76). q029, "What is a 'company card'?", has a best score of 0.58, below six of the unanswerable questions, so no threshold on similarity can decide that a question has no answer. That stays with F's model and its checked citations.
  - **pgvector gives the same rankings as the run logs.** With the real vectors loaded by `make vector-index`, exact search in the local database returned the same top 50 chunks, in the same order, as the in-memory search the run logs were made with, for all 60 dev questions and all three chunkings; scores differ by less than 1e-6. `make test-db` passes.
  - **Baseline for J's quality gate:** `results/baseline.json` now holds recall@5 for all 18 runs; the BM25 values are unchanged.

- **Prompt and answer format (#62):** `src/rag/prompt.py` turns a question and any retriever's chunks into a prompt in which every paragraph, point or guidance page is marked with its node ID, so an answer can cite "Article 6(3)" (choices in `DECISIONS.md`). It reads the model's reply in the format `{answerable, answer, citations[{node_id, quote}]}`, and gives the labels and fixed messages a reader sees: "Regulation (EC) No 561/2006, Article 6(3), consolidated version of 31.12.2024", a warning on guidance older than the 2020 Mobility Package, and the messages for a question the documents don't answer and for an answer that fails the checks. Tests show that every line of every chunk in all three chunkings lands in the node that owns it, that the same chunks always give the same prompt, that text such as `</node></passage>` or "ignore the instructions above" stays inside its node, and that replies with missing, extra, repeated or mistyped fields are rejected. No model is called. The manifest now records the adoption date of 2002/15, so the 18 retrieval run logs were made again; their rankings are unchanged.
- **The check of every answer (#63):** `src/rag/validate.py` decides, without a model, whether an answer may be shown. It fails a reply that isn't in the answer format, a refusal that cites anything, an answer that cites nothing, a citation of a node that wasn't among the retrieved passages, and a quote that isn't word for word in the cited node's text; every failure is listed for the run log. Only spacing and the typography of quote marks and dashes are forgiven, so `'carriage by road'` matches the act's `‘carriage by road’`, but `3.5 tonnes` doesn't match `3,5 tonnes` (rules in `DECISIONS.md`). A passing citation carries the act's own text, which is what a reader is shown. Tests on hand-built answers cover each rule passing and failing, including a real quote from 561/2006 Art. 8(1) when that article wasn't retrieved and quotes one character off, and on the real corpus every node's text in all three chunkings passes as a quote of itself. No model is called.
